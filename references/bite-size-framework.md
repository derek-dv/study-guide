# Bite-Size Study Framework & Reference Guide

Use this reference whenever creating or reviewing study notes for a student. Students remember more when complex topics are broken into small, structured pieces with active recall questions.

---

## 1. The 5 Bite-Size Study Rules

1. **No Walls of Text**: Never write paragraphs longer than 3 lines. Break ideas into numbered blocks and short bullet points.
2. **The 25-Word Bullet Rule**: Every bullet point must be **25 words or fewer**. If a bullet is longer, split it into two bullets or trim filler words.
3. **Plain Words First, Technical Terms Second**: Explain the idea in everyday words *before* introducing the textbook term in parentheses or bold text.
4. **Build Insight, Not Just Definitions**: Always explain *why* something happens or *how* pieces connect, rather than listing dictionary definitions.
5. **End with Active Recall**: Always include at least 3 `Q:` and `A:` flashcards at the end so the student can test themselves immediately.

---

## 3. Good vs. Bad Examples

### ❌ Bad Example (Textbook Wall of Text)
> Mitosis is a part of the cell cycle in which replicated chromosomes are separated into two new nuclei. Cell division by mitosis gives rise to genetically identical cells in which the total number of chromosomes is maintained. It is preceded by the S stage of interphase and is often followed by telophase and cytokinesis, which divides the cytoplasm, organelles, and cell membrane of one cell into two new cells containing roughly equal shares of these cellular components. The phases include prophase, prometaphase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase.

**Why it fails for students:**
- Overwhelming paragraph with too many terms at once.
- Hard to scan or memorize quickly.
- No memory hook or self-test questions.

### ✅ Good Example (Bite-Size Study Format)
> # Study Guide: Mitosis (Cell Division)
>
> ## The 1-Sentence Big Picture
> Mitosis is how one body cell copies its DNA and splits into two identical twin cells so you can grow and heal.
>
> ## Bite-Sized Core Concepts
> ### 1. Before Splitting (Interphase)
> - The cell grows and makes an exact photocopy of its DNA before mitosis starts.
> - Without this copy, the new cells would only get half the instructions they need.
>
> ### 2. The 4 Main Steps (PMAT)
> - **Prophase**: DNA packs tightly into visible chromosomes, and the nucleus shell dissolves.
> - **Metaphase**: Chromosomes line up single-file in the **M**iddle of the cell.
> - **Anaphase**: Fibers pull the chromosome copies **A**part to opposite sides.
> - **Telophase**: **T**wo new nuclei form around the separated DNA sets.
>
> ### 3. The Final Cut (Cytokinesis)
> - The rest of the cell pinches in the middle, leaving two identical daughter cells.
>
> ## Real-World Analogy
> Imagine photocopying a recipe book, putting one copy on the left table and one on the right, and building a wall down the middle of the kitchen.
>
> ## Key Insight & Exam Trap
> - **Core Insight**: Just remember **PMAT** (Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase) -> **P**ack, **M**iddle, **A**part, **T**wo.
> - **Watch Out**: Don't confuse **mitosis** (2 identical body cells) with **meiosis** (4 unique sperm/egg cells).
>
> ## Active Recall Flashcards
> Q: What does the memory trick PMAT stand for in mitosis?
> A: Prophase (Pack), Metaphase (Middle), Anaphase (Apart), Telophase (Two).
>
> Q: How do the two new cells after mitosis compare to the original cell?
> A: They are genetically identical and have the exact same number of chromosomes.
>
> Q: When is DNA copied during cell division?
> A: During Interphase, BEFORE mitosis begins.
